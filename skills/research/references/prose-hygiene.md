# Prose hygiene for the report

The report is prose, so it can pick up the default-model tells these rules
cut. They govern Summary/Background/Body sentences — not telegraphic
artifacts (finding lines, graph labels, Unresolved bullets) and not quoted
source text. Curated from humanizer (github.com/blader/humanizer, MIT) and
unslop (github.com/theclaymethod/unslop, MIT); both descend from
Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing".

A tell is a default choice — a deliberate use can stay. When in doubt,
keep the claim, drop the dressing.

## Stage nothing — state it

- **Not X but Y.** "Not just a feature, it's a shift" — the negative half
  names something nobody claimed, so the positive half sounds larger.
  State Y directly. Keep the contrast only when the negative half corrects
  a belief the reader actually holds.
- **Staged run-up.** "Here's what you need to know", "let's dive in", a
  heading restated by its own first sentence — cut to the claim. These
  still slip into openings even though the report bans transitions.
- **One-line closer.** A lone-sentence paragraph restating the section
  ("That distinction matters."). Cut unless it carries a fact the
  paragraph didn't.
- **Arguing with no one.** "This isn't to say", "some might claim" —
  nobody did. Make the claim; if a real source disagrees it earns a
  `contradicts` edge, not a straw man.

## Specific over dressed-up

- **Deep-sounding sayings.** "At its core", "the real question is", "the
  language of" — replace the aphorism with the mechanism.
- **Inflation.** "Groundbreaking", "seamless", "robust" doing evaluative
  work — state what it does. A claim strong enough doesn't need a trumpet.
- **Borrowed authority.** "Experts argue", "studies show" — the report
  cites by number or not at all; unnamed authority is source laundering in
  prose form.
- **is/are/has avoidance.** "Serves as", "boasts", "features" where *is*,
  *are*, *has* says it.
- **Vague declaratives.** "The implications are significant" — name the
  implication or cut the sentence.

## Shape

- **Meta-narration.** "This report examines", "we searched", "compiled
  from" — write about the subject. Coverage accounting already has a home:
  Unresolved.
- **Recap coda.** A closing paragraph restating the Summary — the report
  ends at Sources; don't encore.
- **Metronome rhythm.** Same-length sentences in a row, the same opener
  per paragraph — vary length. Telegraphic prose is uneven by nature;
  staccato is not the goal either — a clause carrying a claim stays.
- **Knowledge-limit filler.** "Not publicly documented", "details are
  limited" padding a body sentence — say what the source doesn't show, in
  Unresolved. Never let a gap become a plausible guess in the body.

## Draft check

Before saving, scan the prose once for: a not-X-but-Y contrast, a fragment
or closer restating its paragraph, a saying standing in for a claim, an
evaluative adjective doing evidence's work, a sentence narrating the
document. Each sighting is a candidate cut — the question is always "what
claim does this sentence carry?"
