# `/learn-companion self-learn`

Independent study with a knowledge check. The learner studied a topic on
their own — their own materials, a course, videos, practice — and you verify
what actually landed before generating practice for it. Self-reported
knowledge isn't demonstrated knowledge; the point of this command is to
measure before trusting, and to aim the quiz at what the measurement finds.

## Workflow

1. Scope what they claim to have learned: topic, material used, and how far
   they got. If the topic isn't on the roadmap, that's fine — self-learn
   covers independent study, not just companion-taught material. Add it to
   the roadmap only if the learner wants to keep it.
2. Judge before you quiz. Run a short diagnostic — 3–5 questions spanning
   the claimed scope, ordered easy to hard, asking them to explain or apply
   rather than self-rate. Their answers map the terrain per sub-topic:
   solid, shaky, or absent. A confident self-report that can't produce an
   answer is data, not embarrassment — report it factually.
3. Generate the quiz weighted toward what the diagnostic exposed — shaky
   and absent areas get the volume; solid areas get one confirmation item
   at most. A uniform sweep wastes retrieval effort on what's already
   known. One question at a time, committed answer before feedback, mixed
   formats (recall, apply, explain-why, predict).
4. Record per sub-topic: demonstrated, assisted, or absent in
   `progress.md`. Weak areas go to Needs reinforcement and route back to
   `study`. Set `last reviewed` to today and `next due` per the spacing
   rule — the diagnostic *was* a retrieval attempt, so the schedule starts
   now.
5. Before ending a turn that awaits a learner answer, write the
   `## Pending decision` section in `progress.md` — the posed question
   and the awaited answer. Report the map plainly: what's solid, what's shaky, what's missing, and
   the recommended next step. No softening — a diagnostic the learner can
   argue with is more useful than one that flatters.

If they haven't studied yet and are asking where to start, point them at a
scope and a resource type rather than quizzing an untaught topic — the
diagnostic only works on material they've actually covered.
