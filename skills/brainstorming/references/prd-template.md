# PRD Template

Use when the user chooses "PRD" as the output format in Phase 3. Adapt sections to the topic — drop ones that don't apply, don't force-fill.

## Structure

1. **Problem** — one paragraph. What's broken or missing today, who feels it, why it matters now.
2. **Users** — who this is for. Primary, secondary, and who is affected but not served. Be specific ("on-call engineers at companies with >50 services"), not generic ("developers").
3. **Solution** — the chosen direction from Phase 3, described concretely. What it does, how it works at a level a new teammate could follow. Not a feature list — a coherent explanation.
4. **Success criteria** — how you'll know it worked in 3–6 months. Measurable if possible, but honest qualitative signals are fine if the metric would be theater.
5. **Scope** — what's in, what's explicitly out. The "out" list matters as much as the "in" list; it's what prevents scope creep later.
6. **Risks & open questions** — the honest weaknesses from Phase 2, plus anything still unresolved. Don't hide these — they're the most useful part of the doc for a reviewer.
7. **Alternatives considered** — the other directions from Phase 2, one line each, with why they were rejected. This is the audit trail of the decision.

## Why this structure

The order matters: problem before solution forces the reader to understand the *why* before evaluating the *what*. Success criteria before scope prevents the scope list from becoming the spec. Risks and alternatives at the end are not appendix material — they're the decision record.
