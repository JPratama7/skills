# Idea Sharpening Prompts

Questions for Session flow, step 1. Pick the smallest set that gets the user
to a concrete one-sentence takeaway. Stop asking the moment the takeaway is
sharp — these are probes, not a questionnaire.

## Core probe (always ask first)

"In one sentence: what will the reader know or be able to do after this post
that they couldn't before?"

## Sharpening follow-ups (route by the failure)

If the takeaway is vague ("X is interesting / important"):
- "Interesting to whom — and what changes for them on Monday morning?"
- "What's the sentence a reader would quote to a coworker?"

If the takeaway is too broad ("everything about task queues"):
- "What's the one decision in this space you have an opinion on that the
  docs don't settle?"
- "Which post would you rather have written: the survey, or the one mistake
  you'll never make again? Write that one."

If the takeaway is a tutorial ("how to set up X"):
- "What did the official docs get wrong or omit that cost you time?"
- "What broke when you did this in production?"

If the takeaway is missing entirely (exploration disguised as a post):
- "Are you writing to teach, to announce, or to think out loud? The first
  two need a claim; the third needs a question — which is it?"

## Angle finders (technical posts)

- The failure story: "What did you believe before this broke?"
- The number: "What's the measurement that would surprise people?"
- The counterexample: "When is the standard advice wrong?"
- The decision: "What did you choose, what did you reject, and why?"

## Title stress-test

Once a title exists, ask:
1. "Would a reader know exactly what they'll get from the title alone?"
2. "Does the title make a claim the post actually defends?"
3. "Would you click this if someone else published it?" (the click test —
   if no, treat as dread/avoidance per the block playbook)
